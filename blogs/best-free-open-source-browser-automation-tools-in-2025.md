<h2 id="what-is-browser-automation-and-why-small-teams-need-it">What is Browser Automation and Why Small Teams Need It</h2>



Browser automation refers to using software to automatically perform user tasks like moving through web pages, filling forms, and extracting data without manual intervention. For small teams juggling multiple repetitive tasks across different websites, this technology eliminates tedious manual work and dramatically increases productivity.

Small teams face unique challenges that make browser automation particularly valuable. You're often wearing multiple hats, managing tight budgets, and dealing with repetitive tasks that eat up precious time. Whether you're downloading invoices from vendor portals, filling out government forms, or extracting data from competitor websites, these manual processes can consume hours each week.

The portal burden shows up differently across verticals, though the underlying problem is the same: critical workflows trapped in websites with no API. Healthcare teams manage insurance eligibility verification and prior authorization across 10-40+ payer portals, each with its own login flow and form structure. Legal practices work through court e-filing systems (PACER, CM/ECF, Tyler Technologies platforms) where jurisdiction-specific requirements mean the same filing process looks different in every court. Government contractors handle state tax filings across all 50 states, a workflow that can reach ~95% unattended automation when the tooling can adapt to state-by-state layout variations. Insurance agencies juggle 15-40 carrier portals for policy management and quoting, where parallel automation finishes in ~20 minutes what sequential manual work stretches into 6+ hours.

The beauty of modern browser automation lies in its accessibility. Unlike enterprise solutions that require dedicated IT teams, modern tools can be implemented by small teams without major infrastructure investment. Free and open source solutions have matured to the point where they rival expensive commercial alternatives.

For teams dealing with form automation needs, the time savings become even more dramatic. Instead of manually moving through dozens of similar forms across different websites, automation handles these tasks consistently and accurately.

The <a href="https://github.com/angrykoala/awesome-browser-automation?ref=skyvern.com" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">complete browser automation resources</a> available today show just how strong this ecosystem has become, with tools suitable for every skill level and use case.



<h2 id="understanding-agentic-process-automation-apa">Understanding Agentic Process Automation (APA)</h2>



Before we get into specific tools, it helps to understand where browser automation fits in the larger picture. Agentic Process Automation is the discipline of automating complex, multi-step business processes using AI agents that can perceive, reason, plan, execute, and adapt, without requiring pre-coded rules for every decision.

Think about the business workflows your team still handles manually. Eligibility checks across payer portals. Tax filings that vary state by state. Invoice downloads from vendor systems. Court submissions with jurisdiction-specific requirements. These processes share a common problem: they run on portals that have no API, change their layouts without warning, and require human judgment to handle exceptions.

That's the class of problem Agentic Process Automation platforms are built for: browser execution is the mechanism, but autonomous multi-step operation, exception handling, and audit trails are the actual product. Browser automation handles the clicks and form fills. APA handles the reasoning about what to click, when to escalate, and how to recover when something unexpected happens.

Skyvern sits at the execution layer of this growing category. Where traditional browser automation tools require you to hard-code every selector and decision path upfront, Skyvern uses computer vision and LLMs to read pages visually at runtime and work through workflows it's never seen before. This makes it the execution-layer specialist for portal-based work, bridging high-level process orchestration and the actual browser interaction that carries it out.

The distinction matters because it changes what you're buying. Traditional browser automation gives you infrastructure. APA platforms give you goal-directed workflows that can handle the messy reality of production operations: authentication flows that change, forms with conditional logic, portals that rate-limit or throw errors, and compliance requirements that demand audit trails.



<h2 id="how-to-choose-the-right-browser-automation-tool-for-your-small-team">How to Choose the Right Browser Automation Tool for Your Small Team</h2>



Start by assessing your team's technical expertise and available time for implementation and maintenance. A powerful tool that nobody can properly implement or maintain won't solve your problems.



<h3 id="apa-vs-traditional-rpa">APA vs. Traditional RPA</h3>



Traditional RPA platforms (UiPath, Automation Anywhere, Blue Prism) were built for enterprise IT departments with dedicated automation engineers. They require certified developers who build workflows using brittle XPath selectors that break the moment a portal renames a button or restructures a layout. When a site changes, someone needs to open the automation studio, debug the broken selector, update the script, and redeploy. That maintenance burden compounds: each new portal adds another point of failure, and each layout change triggers another fix cycle.

The provisioning overhead runs deeper than code maintenance. Traditional RPA requires queue management, bot orchestration, and capacity planning infrastructure. Automating a workflow also means managing a fleet of virtual workers that need scheduling, monitoring, and exception handling at the infrastructure layer.

Agentic Process Automation platforms like Skyvern eliminate that entire layer. Non-technical teams can build automations in minutes through simple API calls or visual workflow builders. Where a selector breaks on every portal update, Skyvern re-reads the page visually at runtime and keeps going. The self-healing capability means you set up the automation once and it adapts automatically as websites evolve, with no emergency fixes when a critical vendor portal changes their interface.

The distinction matters most for small teams. You likely don't have certified RPA developers on staff, and the cost of hiring them is hard to defend. APA platforms bring powerful automation within reach of operations teams who understand the workflow but don't write code.

**Define your primary use case:** Are you automating business processes, testing web applications, or extracting data? Different tools excel at different tasks. Government form automation requires different features than web scraping or testing.

**Consider your technical skills:** If your team consists primarily of business users with limited programming experience, code-heavy solutions like Selenium will create more problems than they solve. Conversely, if you have experienced developers, you might prefer the control that traditional tools provide.

**Check maintenance overhead:** Traditional automation tools require ongoing maintenance as websites change. For small teams, this can become a major burden. AI-powered solutions like Skyvern reduce this overhead by adapting automatically to website changes.

**Think about scalability:** Your automation needs will likely grow over time. Choose tools that can scale with your team, not solutions you'll outgrow quickly.

For teams dealing with invoice processing automation or similar repetitive tasks, focus on tools that can handle multiple websites with minimal configuration. The ability to work across different sites without custom coding becomes important as your automation needs expand.

<h2 id="2-old-school-python-option-selenium">2. Old School Python Option: Selenium</h2>





<figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://dcbllm8dvghjo.cloudfront.net/media/blog/81d66d5952e32e5c2404299f86eb6aea5aa53fa104b064b1dcbf619dbfe563ec-i5vzty6z7ix-1lnlaflre.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy"></figure>



Selenium remains one of the most widely used automation testing tools for web applications. It supports multiple browsers and operating systems, making it incredibly versatile for cross-browser testing. As the oldest player in browser automation with the longest track record, Selenium has earned its reputation as the de facto standard.

**Key strengths:**

-   Supports multiple programming languages (Java, C#, Python, Ruby, JavaScript)
-   Cross-browser testing across all major browsers and operating systems
-   Massive community and extensive documentation
-   Mature ecosystem with countless plugins and extensions

But Selenium requires programming knowledge and understanding of browser drivers and testing frameworks. The setup can be complex compared to modern all-in-one solutions, with a steep learning curve for beginners.

The maintenance overhead is substantial. When websites change their structure, you'll spend time updating XPath selectors and debugging broken scripts. For small teams without dedicated developers, this can become a major burden.

Selenium works best when you have experienced developers who can architect solid automation frameworks and maintain them over time. If that describes your team, Selenium offers maximum control and flexibility.

**Best for:** Developer-heavy teams that need maximum control and cross-browser compatibility.



<h2 id="3-another-legacy-solution-playwright">3. Another Legacy Solution: Playwright</h2>





<figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://dcbllm8dvghjo.cloudfront.net/media/blog/a5bc98fdb97f96ac5093f54b0597cad0a20620e10abcf056b3266f63098f2dcb-5ga55vffrwmrpdz-gn5mg.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy"></figure>



Developed by Microsoft, Playwright is the next generation of web automation tools. It runs tests across Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit with identical code, providing true end-to-end cross-browser testing features.

**Key strengths:**

-   Sophisticated auto-wait mechanism that automatically waits for elements to be actionable
-   Multi-language support across JavaScript/TypeScript, Python, Java, and .NET
-   Modern architecture designed for today's web applications
-   Excellent debugging and development tools

Playwright tackles many of Selenium's pain points with a more modern approach. The auto-wait functionality alone eliminates countless timing issues that plague traditional automation scripts.

However, Playwright's community is smaller than Selenium's, though it has grown quickly since its 2020 launch. You'll find fewer third-party integrations and community-contributed solutions.

The learning curve is moderate. More approachable than Selenium but still requires solid programming skills. Documentation is excellent, but you might find fewer Stack Overflow answers for edge cases.

**Best for:** Development teams wanting modern automation tools with excellent cross-browser support.



<h2 id="4-chrome-limited-tool-puppeteer">4. Chrome-Limited Tool: Puppeteer</h2>





<figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://dcbllm8dvghjo.cloudfront.net/media/blog/604031ef30f4fcb815d6f0d4e00869429080b74487e113e1741e2f6d0c89b975-bkulbxhjzc8smi373l4ol.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy"></figure>



Born inside Google, Puppeteer provides a high-level API to control Chrome and Chromium-based browsers. It communicates directly with browsers using the Chrome DevTools Protocol, making it fundamentally an automation tool, not a testing framework.

**Key strengths:**

-   Incredible speed and reliability by avoiding separate driver overhead
-   Simple setup as a Node.js package with automatic Chromium download
-   Excellent for PDF generation and web scraping tasks
-   Direct integration with Chrome's debugging tools

The speed advantage is real. By eliminating the WebDriver layer, Puppeteer often performs much faster than Selenium-based solutions. For a deeper look at how the two compare on performance, see this <a href="https://www.skyvern.com/blog/puppeteer-vs-playwright-complete-performance-comparison-2025/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Puppeteer vs. Playwright performance breakdown</a>. Setup is remarkably simple if you're already in the Node.js ecosystem.

But the browser limitation is major. Officially supporting only Chromium-based browsers means you can't test cross-browser compatibility. For many small teams, this constraint eliminates Puppeteer from consideration.

The JavaScript-only limitation also narrows its appeal. If your team works primarily in other languages, Puppeteer won't fit naturally into your workflow.

**Best for:** JavaScript teams focused on Chrome automation and web scraping.



<h2 id="5-test-focused-platform-cypress">5. Test-Focused Platform: Cypress</h2>





<figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://dcbllm8dvghjo.cloudfront.net/media/blog/a77fc3d14791443987770dacf8a062c3dd6a84fa084dc0b55b4e8d79c6a5ade9-lcz5xjqgmuh7guacayd2s.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy"></figure>



Cypress is an open-source testing platform known for its simple setup and developer-friendly interface. It runs tests directly in the browser, making debugging faster and more intuitive than traditional automation tools.

**Key strengths:**

-   Automatically waits for commands and assertions before proceeding
-   Delivers faster, more consistent results without WebDriver dependencies
-   Excellent debugging experience with time-travel features
-   Strong focus on developer experience and ease of use

The debugging experience really sets Cypress apart. When tests fail, you can see exactly what happened at each step, making troubleshooting much more straightforward than with other tools.

However, Cypress has some major limitations. It emulates mobile devices using desktop browsers instead of testing on actual devices. The focus on testing also means it's less suitable for general automation tasks like data extraction or form filling.

Browser support, while improving, still lags behind more mature tools. The architecture also imposes some constraints on certain types of testing scenarios.

**Best for:** Teams primarily focused on web application testing with strong JavaScript skills.



<h2 id="6-serverless-infrastructure-option-browserbase">6. Serverless Infrastructure Option: Browserbase</h2>





<figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://dcbllm8dvghjo.cloudfront.net/media/blog/5edb261ffc2497b97022a25e5da7e6b07e82abe1e66390cb8c10bcfb3add9961-4fw7o8ipxb5jblrlb37ru.webp" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy"></figure>



Browserbase provides serverless headless browser infrastructure with built-in debugging, session recording, and stealth capabilities. It handles the browser execution layer (managing browser instances, proxies, and anti-detection), but you still write the automation logic yourself using Playwright or Puppeteer.

**Key strengths:**

-   Managed browser infrastructure eliminates DevOps overhead for scaling
-   Built-in session recording and debugging tools for troubleshooting
-   Stealth mode and proxy rotation to bypass basic bot detection
-   Integrates with existing Playwright and Puppeteer code

The infrastructure-as-a-service approach means you don't manage browser instances, handle proxy rotation, or debug headless environments yourself. Browserbase takes care of the execution environment while you focus on the automation logic.

But you're still writing and maintaining selectors. When a portal changes its layout, you're debugging XPath queries and updating your scripts. The infrastructure stays reliable, but the automation logic remains brittle.

For teams with developers who want to write automation code but don't want to manage browser infrastructure, Browserbase splits the difference. You get control over the logic without the overhead of running headless browsers at scale.

**Best for:** Developer teams that want to write their own automation logic but need managed browser infrastructure at scale.

-   Flexible architecture for custom agent workflows

For development teams building agentic automation or custom browser agents for internal tools, Browser Use provides a solid foundation. But teams needing production-ready automation for portal-heavy workflows will spend considerable time bridging the gap between framework and production system.

<h2 id="8-managed-browser-option-hyperbrowser-ai">8. Managed Browser Option: Hyperbrowser AI</h2>

Hyperbrowser AI offers managed headless browsers with built-in CAPTCHA solving and anti-detection capabilities. The service handles browser infrastructure and common blocking mechanisms, but you still build the automation logic using their API or integration with existing tools.

**Key strengths:**

-   Built-in CAPTCHA solving reduces manual intervention
-   Anti-detection features for sites with bot protection
-   Managed infrastructure eliminates browser maintenance

The CAPTCHA solving capability handles a major pain point for teams automating sites with reCAPTCHA or similar protections. Instead of manually solving challenges or integrating third-party CAPTCHA services, Hyperbrowser handles it as part of the infrastructure.

But the automation logic still lives in your code. You're writing selectors, handling navigation flows, and maintaining scripts when sites change their structure. Hyperbrowser solves the infrastructure and anti-bot layer, not the workflow automation layer.

For teams automating a small number of well-understood sites that deploy CAPTCHA protection, Hyperbrowser removes specific friction points. Teams dealing with portal sprawl across dozens of sites with changing layouts will still face the maintenance burden of selector-based automation.

<h2 id="9-read-only-scraping-tool-firecrawl">9. Read-Only Scraping Tool: Firecrawl</h2>





<figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="https://dcbllm8dvghjo.cloudfront.net/media/blog/9a7e4e1cd2f2e54a54f5ad783938944317160004b5f220ecf803b53575381156-5eolwen4owypxqvqvja-z.png" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy"></figure>



Firecrawl is an API-first web scraping tool that extracts clean, structured data from websites. It handles dynamic JavaScript pages, returns markdown or structured JSON, and works well for content extraction tasks, but it doesn't fill forms, click buttons, or execute multi-step workflows.

**Key strengths:**

-   Simple API interface for quick integration
-   Returns clean markdown or structured JSON output
-   Handles JavaScript-heavy sites that traditional scrapers miss
-   Good documentation and developer experience

For read-only data extraction (monitoring competitor pricing, pulling product catalogs, extracting article content), Firecrawl delivers clean results without the overhead of browser automation. The API abstraction means you don't manage browser instances or debug page-load issues.

The limitation is right in the name: this is web scraping, not workflow automation. You can't log in to a portal, fill out a quote form, download an invoice, or work through multi-step processes. Firecrawl reads what's already on the page; it doesn't interact with it.

Teams building content aggregation tools, competitive intelligence dashboards, or data pipelines that consume public web data will find Firecrawl handles the extraction layer cleanly. Teams needing to automate core workflows (eligibility checks, tax filings, invoice downloads) need a tool that can write to sites as well as read from them.

**Best for:** Teams extracting data from public websites where no form interaction or authentication is required.
