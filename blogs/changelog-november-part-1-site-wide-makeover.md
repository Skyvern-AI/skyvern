<h2 id="landing-page-re-design">Landing page re-design</h2>





<p>We just released our new landing page at <a href="https://www.skyvern.com" rel="noreferrer">https://www.skyvern.com</a> <br><br>Our old one desperately needed a makeover. It was something I cooked up two nights before a launch. <br><br>Even though it looked scrappy, it served its purpose well. <br><br>Our potential customers were able to go to it and see what Skyvern could do. They would see use-cases, they'd be able to book a demo, they'd be able to try our product out, they'd be able to navigate to our open source repo<br><br>There are a few places where it really fell short:<br>1. I built it in Webflow, and it was becoming challenging to make non-trivial updates to it<br>2. All of the images were AI generated, and some poorly at that. They didn't have any intrinsic visual appeal (and sometimes I felt like it hurt more than it helped)<br>3. We wanted to migrate to Framer and were looking for an excuse to invest the time -- turns out prepping for a Hackernews launch is the way to do it<br>4. We wanted to add some testimonials to our website<br>5. We wanted to highlight that we were open source!<br><br>So.. here we are. New launch. New landing page.<br><br>I'd love to hear what you think! Anything we could improve?</p>

<h2 id="task-page-re-design">Task Page re-design</h2>



The old one looked and felt like an MVP.. because it was one :)

It had a few major issues:

1.  It didn't segment the sections correctly, overwhelming the user
2.  It didn't have adequate help text, forcing our users to carry a higher mental burden understanding how to use Skyvern
3.  The field formatting itself feels more crisp now, increasing the perceived quality of the product
