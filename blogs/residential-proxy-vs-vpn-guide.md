<h2 id="what-is-a-residential-proxy">What Is a Residential Proxy</h2>



A residential proxy is an intermediary server that uses IP locations assigned by Internet Service Providers (ISPs) to real residential devices like home routers, computers, and mobile phones. Unlike datacenter proxies that originate from commercial servers, residential proxies route your internet traffic through genuine household internet connections.

Think of it as borrowing someone else's internet connection temporarily. When you make a request through a residential proxy, websites see the IP location of an actual home user instead of a commercial server. This makes your traffic appear completely natural and legitimate.

The key difference lies in IP reputation. Residential IP locations have built browsing histories and are trusted by websites because they belong to real people. <h2 id="how-residential-proxies-work">How Residential Proxies Work</h2>



When you connect through a residential proxy, your requests don’t go straight from your computer to the website. Instead, they first pass through the proxy provider, which routes them through a real residential device (like a home router or phone) before reaching the site.

To the destination website, it looks like the traffic is coming from a regular household internet connection. That makes the request appear natural and helps bypass detection systems that usually block datacenter traffic.

Providers let you choose the IP location, so your requests can appear to come from a specific country, city, or even neighborhood. You can also rotate IPs automatically to avoid detection or use “sticky” sessions that hold the same IP longer when you need a consistent identity.

The process is nearly invisible. Connections may be slightly slower than a direct request, but the payoff is much higher success rates for scraping, ad verification, or multi-region testing.

<h2 id="residential-proxy-vs-vpn">Residential Proxy vs VPN</h2>



Residential proxies and VPNs often get compared, but they solve different problems. A VPN encrypts all your traffic and routes it through a secure tunnel, which is ideal for personal privacy, remote work, or sensitive <a href="https://www.skyvern.com/government?ref=skyvern.com" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">government workflows</a> where data security is non-negotiable. The tradeoff is slower speeds and the fact that many websites block known VPN IP ranges.

Residential proxies don’t focus on full-device security. Instead, they let you mask your IP on a per-app or per-request basis, using real home connections that are much harder to detect. That makes them better suited for business automation, scraping, ad verification, and regional testing. They can be used anywhere you need reliable access at scale instead of blanket encryption.

In short: VPNs excel when privacy and secure communication are the priority, while residential proxies shine when success rates and trusted IPs matter most.

<h3 id="what-is-the-main-difference-between-residential-proxies-and-datacenter-proxies">What is the main difference between residential proxies and datacenter proxies?</h3>



Residential proxies use IP locations from real home internet connections provided by ISPs, while datacenter proxies come from commercial servers. This makes residential proxies appear more legitimate to websites and much harder to detect or block.



<h3 id="how-do-i-choose-between-rotating-and-static-residential-proxies">How do I choose between rotating and static residential proxies?</h3>



Use rotating proxies for large-scale web scraping and data collection where you need to avoid rate limiting. Choose static proxies when you need consistent identity across sessions, such as managing accounts or accessing services that flag frequent IP changes.



<h3 id="can-i-use-free-residential-proxies-for-business-automation">Can I use free residential proxies for business automation?</h3>

Choose residential proxies when you need granular control over specific applications, better performance for web scraping, or higher success rates accessing restricted websites. Use VPNs when you need complete security and encryption for all your internet traffic.
